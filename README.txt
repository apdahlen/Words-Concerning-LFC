Consider:

Veith gives an ecclesial vocation. Sverdrup gives a vocational ecclesiology.

That is much bigger.

For Sverdrup, the activity is not something appended to church membership. Activity belongs to what the congregation is. Your Sverdrup quotation says exactly that:

“By congregation we mean a gathering of active Christians…”

Therefore an inactive congregation is not merely performing poorly. It is failing to manifest its own nature.

And this clarifies why “Kingdom of God on earth” has so much explosive force in your argument. If modern readers hear it merely as an elevated synonym for “church,” almost everything disappears. Sverdrup seems to mean something closer to:

Christ's reign becomes visibly operative here, through this gathered body and the gifts and work of its members.

That also explains why Berlie can move so naturally from grace to work. The Christian has not merely been saved from something and then assigned several unrelated vocations. He has been incorporated into a living body that itself has a God-given purpose in the world.

So I think your criticism of Veith becomes more precise:

Veith helps explain the Christian's vocation in God's world. Sverdrup insists that the Christian has also been incorporated into a visible manifestation of God's Kingdom that itself acts.

Without that second claim, “church vocation” can mean little more than attend, receive, perhaps volunteer.

With Sverdrup, it means:

you are part of something Christ has constituted to act. Therefore its work is, in some measure, your work.

That is the piece you are recovering.
