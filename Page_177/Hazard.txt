STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* “Frikirke” denotes a Free Church: a church constituted independently of state-church control and external ecclesiastical compulsion. It should not be reduced to the vaguer “independent church.”

* “opgave” means “task,” but here it also carries the weight of a divinely entrusted responsibility. When paired with “kald,” the two should remain “calling and task” rather than being collapsed into “mission.”

* “naa et stykke paa vei” is idiomatic: to advance some distance or make some progress toward a goal.

* “i sin vorden” means “in its becoming,” hence still in a formative or developing stage. “In its infancy” is possible but may overstate institutional immaturity.

* “fjernsyn” predates the modern meaning “television.” Here it denotes a distant view or something seen from afar.

* “stævne henimot” is directional and purposeful: to set one’s course toward something.

* “indklemt” means hemmed in or pressed between surrounding bodies.

* “berettigelse og hævd” is a paired expression combining recognized legitimacy with standing established through long possession or custom.

* “ufortrødent” means steadfastly, undauntedly, or without becoming discouraged.

* “præsteskole” is best rendered “seminary” in this ecclesiastical context.

* “frimodig” carries biblical and devotional overtones of confidence or boldness grounded in assurance, not mere personal courage.

* “den like vei” means the straight or direct path, not an “equal” way.

* “erkjendelse” can mean recognition, understanding, or settled conviction. In this text, the correct English term depends upon whether intellectual apprehension or inward certainty is foremost.

* “faa os til at drive” evokes drifting from a set course. The maritime image should not be flattened into merely “defeat us.”

* “gaa glip av” means to forfeit or miss what had been intended for one.

* “frikirketanken” is the Free Church idea or principle: the ecclesiological conviction represented by free congregations.

* “kirketrængere” is historically and culturally difficult. It appears to denote Norwegian people seeking, needing, or striving toward a church home. “Norwegian seekers after a church home” preserves the practical and ecclesial force without inventing a technical category.

* “levendegjørelse” is more than administrative renewal. It means a bringing to life and may carry spiritual-resurrection overtones.

* “partigjærder” literally means party or factional fences: barriers formed by parties and divisions.

* “satser” may mean propositions, formulas, or formally stated principles. It should not automatically be rendered “doctrines,” since that could make the author appear to reject doctrine itself.

* “søskendflok” is a family or gathered company of brothers and sisters.

* “ve og vel” is a fixed expression concerning welfare, well-being, or future condition.

* “arbeidslyst” means willingness or eagerness to work, not merely industriousness.

* “kirkelige hjem” means the communal church home being built by the members themselves.

Syntactic Hazards

* The prose advances through long cumulative sentences in which qualifications and consequences are added clause by clause.

* Semicolons and em dashes often mark genuine logical pivots and must not be replaced indiscriminately by short sentences.

* Repeated constructions such as “Vi trænger” establish sermonic anaphora. Their repetition is deliberate and should remain visible.

* Several sentences contain parenthetical qualifications that prevent overstatement, especially the qualification that the present members of the Free Church are not themselves unconquerable.

* The questions concerning what the Church needs introduce the central argument. They must retain their rhetorical function rather than becoming headings or paraphrased assertions.

* The sentence spanning pages 178–179 suspends its main conclusion while inserting the condition governing the Church’s confidence. This cumulative structure should remain intact.

* The pronoun sequence “mit, den enkeltes” and “min, vor menighets” moves deliberately from personal responsibility to congregational responsibility. Smoothing it too much would weaken the self-implicating force.

Theological Hazards

* The distinction between institutional necessities and the supreme necessity of the Spirit of God is the doctrinal center of the article.

* “Guds aand” should be rendered “the Spirit of God,” not merely “spirituality,” enthusiasm, or religious spirit.

* The Spirit’s work is described as testimony concerning Jesus, guidance, illumination through Scripture, and the giving of strength. These functions must remain personal and theological.

* “Jesus er vort liv” is not merely inspirational. It identifies Jesus as the life of both the believer and the congregation.

* The claim that the Free Church’s position is biblical is grounded in the word of Scripture, not in organizational preference.

* “uovervindelig” applies ultimately to the biblical Free Church principle, not to the present organization or its members.

* The possibility that God will raise up others if the present members fail must retain its warning concerning forfeited blessing.

* The author’s affirmation of believing congregations within other church bodies must remain intact. His ecclesiological claim is strong, but it is not a denial that genuine believers exist elsewhere.

* “den rette form for Guds rike paa jorden” is an uncompromising ecclesiological assertion and should not be softened into “one useful expression of Christian community.”

* Brotherly love is presented as the inward bond replacing external compulsion. It is not an optional virtue added to organizational structures.

* Responsibility is individualized: the Church’s future depends upon my relation to it and upon the relation of my congregation, not first upon the failures of others.

Voice Hazards

* The author speaks with institutional sobriety, pastoral earnestness, and moral pressure. A promotional or celebratory tone would distort the article.

* His hope is never detached from labor, perseverance, responsibility, and possible failure.

* The recurring “we need” must sound like confession and summons, not like a strategic planning checklist.

* The transitions “But,” “No,” “On the contrary,” and “And finally—and not least” carry argumentative force and should remain prominent.

* The warning that the present Free Church may fail is deliberately severe. It must not be softened by reassuring commentary.

* The final appeal implicates every reader according to ability and gifts. The conclusion is not merely that work ought to be done, but that each person must fill the place assigned by the Lord.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “naa et stykke paa vei henimot løsningen” means to make some real, though incomplete, progress toward accomplishing the task. A literal “reach a piece on the way” would be unintelligible.

* “i sin vorden” presents the Church as something still coming into developed existence. “Still in its formative stage” preserves this process.

* “se maalet som i et fjernsyn” means to see the goal from afar or in distant prospect. It must not be translated with any modern reference to television.

* “ha blikket fæstet ved det” is the image of fixing one’s gaze upon the goal.

* “stævne henimot det” extends the image from vision to directed movement: the Church must continually set its course toward the goal.

* “indklemt mellem” means hemmed in between more powerful institutions.

* “med penge og magt” is a compressed pairing of material resources and institutional influence.

* “La os gjøre det gode og ikke bli trætte; ti vi skal høste i sin tid, saafremt vi ikke gaar træt” quotes or closely reproduces Galatians 6:9. The King James wording “not be weary in well doing” is a natural historical parallel, but the translation should use modern pronouns and avoid importing unnecessary archaism.

* “ligger det nær” means that something naturally suggests itself or is natural to ask.

* “melder sig for vor tanke” means that answers present themselves to the mind.

* “Nu ja” acknowledges a point without conceding the whole argument: “Well, yes” or “Indeed, it is true.”

* “Ikke ved magt, ikke ved kraft, men ved min aand” is Zechariah 4:6. The familiar English structure “Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit” supplies a natural equivalent, though “not by might, not by power” more closely preserves the Norwegian repetition.

* “først og fremst” means first and foremost, establishing priority rather than exclusivity.

* “se veien” and “vandre den” form a biblical path metaphor: the Spirit enables both discernment and obedient movement.

* “den like vei” is the straight path or direct course consistent with the New Testament understanding already received.

* “faa os til at drive” continues the directional metaphor by warning that the present members may drift from their course in the conflict.

* “gaa glip av den velsignelse” means to forfeit the blessing intended for them, not merely to overlook it accidentally.

* “staa frem utrustet med det lys og den kraft” means to arise furnished by God with the illumination and strength required for the work.

* “Aandens enhet i fredens samband” is a direct echo of Ephesians 4:3. “The unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace” is the natural English biblical equivalent and does not require archaic pronouns.

* “faar lys over” means to receive illumination concerning the meaning, greatness, and beauty of the task.

* “partigjærder” retains a concrete image of fences erected by factions. Translating it merely as “disagreement” would erase the image.

* “broderkjærlighetens sterke, hellige baand” presents love as the powerful and holy bond that holds the Church together from within.

* “ve og vel” concerns the Church’s welfare and future condition.

* “alle med” means that all must take part rather than leaving the work to leaders.

* “fylde den plads” means to occupy faithfully the place or function assigned by the Lord.

* “gjøre gjerningen” is a devotional expression for carrying out the work entrusted by God.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Opening thesis: The Free Church’s task will require long and arduous labor.

* First supporting claim: The Free Church remains in a formative stage, and the goal is still distant.

* Exhortational consequence: Distance from the goal does not permit loss of vision; the Church must keep its gaze fixed and continue moving toward it.

* Description of obstacles: The Church is externally hemmed in by strong, wealthy, established church bodies.

* Deeper diagnosis: The materialistic spirit of the age is also an obstacle.

* Doctrinal pivot: If the calling and task are entrusted by the Lord, difficulty does not alter the obligation to labor.

* Scriptural confirmation: Galatians 6:9 establishes perseverance and promised harvest.

* Governing question: What does the Free Church especially need in order to work purposefully toward its task?

* Preliminary answers: Seminary, money, and gifted leaders are necessary.

* Corrective assertion: Necessary institutional resources are not sufficient.

* Central answer: The Free Church needs above all the Spirit of God.

* Expansion of the central answer: The Spirit bears witness that Jesus is the life of believer and congregation, reveals the way, and gives strength to walk it.

* Scriptural-ecclesiological claim: Through Scripture, the Spirit must produce conviction that the Free Church’s position and foundations are biblical.

* Consequent assurance: The biblical Free Church principle is unconquerable so long as the Church follows the straight New Testament path.

* Necessary qualification: The present members and present organization are not themselves unconquerable.

* Warning: They are weak, vulnerable, and capable of drifting from the struggle.

* Severe providential assertion: If they fail their calling, God can raise up others and continue realizing the Free Church principle without them.

* Clarification concerning other churches: The argument does not deny the value, legitimacy, believing members, or outward effectiveness of other church bodies.

* Positive explanation of Free Church identity: Its members believe that the Lord led them there through historical ecclesiastical developments and that the New Testament points toward free congregations united by the Spirit.

* Restatement of the task: The congregations are to be brought to life and liberated through biblical congregation-building.

* First practical necessity: Brotherly love must form the inward bond where no external bond exists.

* Second practical necessity: Every individual and congregation must bear personal responsibility for the Church’s welfare.

* Warning attached to responsibility: Without such awareness and responsibility, there is no reasonable hope for the Free Church’s future.

* Final practical necessity: All must possess willingness to work.

* Concluding appeal: Each person must, according to ability and gifts, fill the place assigned by the Lord and work there with eagerness, willingness, and joy.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* “Let us do what is good and not grow weary; for we shall reap in due time...” is a direct quotation or close rendering of Galatians 6:9.

* “Not by might, not by power, but by my Spirit, says the Lord” is a direct quotation of Zechariah 4:6.

* “Jesus is our life” closely echoes Colossians 3:4, where Christ is identified as the believer’s life. The author extends the formulation explicitly to the individual and the congregation.

* “The unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace” directly echoes Ephesians 4:3.

* The language of brotherly love making the community recognizable to itself and others likely echoes John 13:35, where disciples are known by their love.

* Love as a strong bond may also recall Colossians 3:14, where love is presented as the bond that unites.

* The appeal that each person fill the place in which the Lord has set him or her recalls the placement of members within the body in 1 Corinthians 12:18.

* The connection between individual gifts and assigned service also resembles 1 Peter 4:10.

* The imagery of seeing a way, walking it, standing firm, drifting, receiving light, and being given strength belongs broadly to biblical wisdom, pilgrimage, and discipleship language, though no single source must be assumed.

* The statement that the word of Scripture remains unshaken despite attack echoes the wider biblical testimony to the permanence and reliability of God’s word.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* The opening acknowledgment that the labor will be “long and arduous” must remain unmitigated.

* The distant goal is not used to excuse delay; it increases the demand for fixed attention and continual movement.

* “We have only one thing to do” must remain categorical. The divine calling eliminates the option of abandoning the work because it is difficult.

* The institutional necessities must be conceded clearly before their insufficiency is declared.

* The assertion “But first and foremost, the Spirit of God” should remain an isolated sentence so that its doctrinal priority is heard.

* The Church’s biblical conviction must be rendered as “full conviction” and “full assurance,” not as probability or preference.

* “Unconquerable” must retain its full force where it applies to the biblical principle.

* The immediate denial that the present members are unconquerable must be equally strong.

* The warning that God may raise up others must preserve the possibility that the present members will forfeit their intended blessing.

* The concession concerning other church bodies should be generous but should not weaken the subsequent assertion concerning free congregations.

* The final movement from collective responsibility to “my own relation” must retain its accusatory and self-examining pressure.

* The closing call must sound obligatory and joyful at once: the reader is summoned to labor, but the labor is to be performed with eagerness and gladness.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* “om vi skal kunne” expresses a real condition of possibility: “if we are to be able.”

* “vil ... bli” states the expected character of the work: it “will be” long and arduous.

* “maa vi ikke tape det av syne” requires “we must not lose sight of it,” not “we should try not to.”

* “har vi bare én ting at gjøre” means “we have only one thing to do.” The obligation is categorical.

* “kan aldrig utrette noget” must remain “can never accomplish anything,” not “may find it difficult.”

* “behøver vi fuld visshet” means “we need full assurance,” not merely “it would help to have confidence.”

* “saalænge vi er villige” establishes a continuing condition: assurance belongs to persistence in the straight path.

* “vil han sørge for” expresses confidence that God will ensure the continuation of the work.

* “skulde ha noget værd” occurs within a denied proposition. It must not be mistranslated as a command that church bodies “ought to have” value.

* “maa jo, om mulig, alle med” retains strong obligation qualified only by practical possibility: “We must, so far as possible, all take part.”

* “prøver at fylde” expresses active endeavor, not passive acceptance of a place.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* “Frikirken” = “the Free Church.”

* “Den lutherske frikirke” = “the Lutheran Free Church.”

* “opgave” = “task.” When paired with “kald,” retain “calling and task.”

* “menighet” = “congregation.”

* “menighetsliv” = “congregational life.”

* “menighetsbyggende arbeide” = “work of building congregations.”

* “kirkesamfund” = “church body” or “church bodies.” In the compound organizational phrase, “denominational organizations” is used for readable English without changing the referent.

* “forening” = “association.”

* “Guds aand” = “the Spirit of God.” It should not be expanded to “Holy Spirit” where the author has not used that exact title.

* “Aanden” in the biblical quotation or Ephesians echo = “the Spirit.”

* “bibelordet” = “the word of Scripture.” This preserves its character as the biblical word rather than reducing it to isolated Bible verses.

* “erkjendelse” = “conviction” when settled ecclesiological certainty is intended; “recognition” when acknowledgment is intended.

* “broderkjærlighet” = “brotherly love,” preserving the historical biblical expression.

* “ansvarsfølelse” = “sense of responsibility.”

* “arbeidslyst” = “willingness to work.”

* “frikirketanken” = “the Free Church principle,” emphasizing that an ecclesiological conviction rather than an abstract notion is intended.

* “lys” = “light” where the scriptural metaphor is active.

* “kraft” = “power” or “strength,” according to whether divine equipment or personal capacity is in view.

* The goal remains distant but visible.

* The Church fixes its gaze upon the goal.

* The Church sets its course toward the goal.

* The Free Church is being built.

* The Church is hemmed in between larger bodies.

* Persevering labor leads to harvest.

* The Spirit shows the way and gives strength to walk it.

* The Church must follow the straight path.

* Weak members may drift in the conflict they have undertaken.

* God equips others with light and power.

* Free congregations are joined by the bond of peace.

* Brotherly love is a strong and holy bond.

* Factions erect fences.

* The members build a church home.

* Each person fills the place assigned by the Lord.

These images should remain images. They should not be converted into explanations about strategy, morale, organizational culture, or institutional sustainability.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Severity: The task is long, arduous, and undertaken under difficult conditions.

* Hope: The goal, though distant, remains visible and must be pursued.

* Severity: Materialism and powerful surrounding church bodies create serious obstacles.

* Consolation and command: A divine calling supplies both the reason and obligation to persevere.

* Practical realism: Seminaries, money, and leaders are genuinely necessary.

* Doctrinal correction: None of these is sufficient without the Spirit of God.

* Confidence: The biblical Free Church principle is unconquerable.

* Humbling qualification: The present members are weak, exposed, and capable of drifting.

* Warning: God’s work may continue through others if the present body fails.

* Fairness toward others: Believing congregations and effective labor exist within other church bodies.

* Renewed conviction: The New Testament points toward free congregations united by the Spirit.

* Warmth: The Church is to be known as a family of brothers and sisters.

* Ethical pressure: Its future depends upon each individual and congregation.

* Joyful obligation: Every member is to labor in the assigned place with eagerness, willingness, and joy.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences are retained where the argument accumulates through conditions and qualifications.

* Short declarations are preserved where the author intends emphasis: “But first and foremost, the Spirit of God.”

* Rhetorical questions remain questions and introduce the article’s central doctrinal answer.

* Repetition of “We need” is preserved as a sermonic refrain.

* Semicolons mark transitions from doctrinal assertion to consequence.

* Em dashes preserve interruption, qualification, and intensified emphasis.

* The repeated balance “not by ... not by ... but by” remains audible.

* The movement from “we,” to “all of us,” to “my own relation,” and finally to “each and every one of us” is preserved.

* Scripture quotations use modern pronouns while retaining parallel structure and gravity.

* The prose is not broken into short contemporary sentences merely for ease of reading.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION

A Few Words on the Lutheran Free Church and Its Task

E. B.

I.

Most of us are surely aware that, if we are to make some progress toward the accomplishment of our task as a Free Church, the labor required for this will be both long and arduous.

I believe we do well to be clear that the Free Church is still in its formative stage. The goal is still far off. But even though we see the goal only from afar, we must not lose sight of it; rather, we must keep our gaze fixed upon it and continually set our course toward it. The conditions under which we labor to build a Free Church are by no means easy. And by this I am not thinking only of the fact that we are hemmed in between large, strong church bodies, with money and power and with long-established legitimacy and standing among our people.

I am also thinking of the materialistic spirit by which our age is so deeply marked. But if the calling and the task have been entrusted to us by the Lord, we have only one thing to do: to labor tirelessly and steadfastly toward the accomplishment of the task, whether the work seems easy or hard to us—according to the word of Scripture: “Let us do what is good and not grow weary; for we shall reap in due time, provided that we do not lose heart.”

For those who have grown to love the Lutheran Free Church in this country, and who therefore seek to take part in its work, it seems natural to me to ask: What is it that we especially need in order to labor purposefully toward the accomplishment of our task? And how can we, if possible, obtain what we need?

I know that many kinds of answers to these questions present themselves to our minds. It is natural, for example, to think of a seminary, much money, great and gifted leaders, and so forth.

Well, yes, it is true: we can never accomplish anything as a Free Church without a seminary, money, and men to lead the work.

But it is equally true that these and many other necessary things are not enough.

And what else—what more—do we need? There is a word from God that in former times was often heard among us, but which in more recent times we may have been in danger of forgetting: “Not by might, not by power, but by my Spirit, says the Lord.”

I will therefore answer the question of what we need most in this way: We need the Spirit—the Spirit of God. It is true that we also need much else, such as brotherly love, a sense of responsibility, and willingness to work.

But first and foremost, the Spirit of God.

We need the Spirit of God to bear witness to us that Jesus is our life—the life of the individual and of the congregation; and we need his guidance both to see the way and to receive strength to walk in it.

We need the help of the Spirit of God, through the word of Scripture, to come to a clear understanding and full conviction that the position of the Free Church is biblical, and that the truths upon which it is built are the very word of Scripture. And the word of Scripture, despite every attack, stands unshaken.

In order to continue with confidence the church work that has been begun, we need full assurance that, so long as we are willing to walk the straight path—in accordance

[Page 179]

with the conviction and understanding we have gained from the New Testament’s presentation of congregational life—the Free Church too is unconquerable, despite all public opinion and all manifest enthusiasm for association.

I have not, however, meant by this to assert that we who today constitute the Lutheran Free Church are unconquerable. No, I am fully aware of our weakness and vulnerability, and that it may not be so difficult to cause us to drift in the struggle we have taken up, which many consider both unnecessary and hopeless.

But my thought is this: if we who now constitute the Free Church fail in our calling and our task, given to us by the Lord, and thus forfeit the blessing he has intended for us through this work, he will see to it that others arise, equipped with the light and power needed for the Free Church principle to be realized more and more, as God’s people gather around the free congregation as the right form of God’s kingdom on earth.

By this I do not mean to say that the ordering and work of denominational organizations have no value or legitimacy. On the contrary. I willingly acknowledge that within the church bodies there are congregations with believing members, and I also willingly acknowledge that a church body, by reason of its strong cohesion, has many favorable conditions for carrying out vigorous work, at least outwardly.

I mean only to say this: that we who belong to the association called the Lutheran Free Church and have learned to love it, belong there because we believe that the Lord, through the ecclesiastical development and ecclesiastical conflicts among Norwegian seekers after a church home in this country, has led us there, and because we believe that the New Testament points in that direction: free congregations joined together by “the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.”

It is therefore also of exceedingly great importance for us that, under the guidance of the Spirit of God, we receive light concerning the greatness and beauty of our task—the bringing of the congregations to life and their liberation—and receive strength to walk the biblical path in our work of building congregations.

We need, in addition—especially in the Free Church, where no outward bonds exist to hold us together—brotherly love, so that we may be bound together as one, not by factional fences and constitution, not by formulas and laws, but by the strong, holy bond of brotherly love, so that it may be recognized and felt, both among ourselves and by others, that we are a family of brothers and sisters.

And we need a sense of responsibility, so that all of us—the individual friends of the Free Church and the individual congregations—continually live and labor under the consciousness that the Lutheran Free Church’s welfare, progress, or decline depends not first and foremost upon the relation of others to it, but upon my own relation to it—the relation of each individual—and upon the relation of my—of our—congregation. Without some measure of such recognition and awareness, and the sense of responsibility that follows from it, it is surely hopeless to expect any future for the Free Church. And finally—and not least—we need willingness to work, so that we are willing to do something to build our church home.

We must, so far as possible, all take part in this work, so that each and every one of us, according to ability and gifts, endeavors to fill the place in which the Lord has set us, and there to do the work for him with eagerness, willingness, and joy.
