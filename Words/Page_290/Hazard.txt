SOME WORDS CONCERNING THE LUTHERAN FREE CHURCH AND ITS TASKS

E. B.

VI.

STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards

* “ræddes tilbake” means to shrink back in fear or hesitation, not merely to be afraid. It carries the moral implication of retreating from an appointed duty.

* “forberedelse” refers not merely to practical preparation but to disciplined spiritual and intellectual preparation for speaking the word.

* “aandelig utvikling” is spiritual development or growth. “Spiritual formation” would introduce a more modern theological idiom.

* “kampdygtighet” literally denotes fitness or readiness for battle. The context is the believer’s struggle against the enemy and must retain its military force.

* “Herrens kjæmpende hær” is the Lord’s fighting or militant army. The phrase belongs to classical Christian warfare imagery.

* “fegten i veiret” is a biblical expression meaning to strike or beat the air without effect. It must not be reduced to “wasted effort.”

* “finde ind i ordet” means to penetrate, enter into, or find one’s way into the word. It implies personal appropriation and understanding, not merely textual study.

* “følelseslivet” and “viljelivet” distinguish the emotional life from the volitional life. The author insists that authentic spiritual work must reach both feeling and will.

* “overfladiske naturer” means superficial persons or dispositions, not merely casual listeners.

* “tilsigter” means consciously aims at, intends, or seeks to accomplish. It is stronger than simply “hopes for.”

* “menneskeros” and “menneskedaddel” are human praise and human blame or censure. The balanced opposition should be retained.

* “bære Jesus Kristus til skue” means to hold or set Jesus Christ openly before others. It has a possible biblical resonance with Christ being publicly set forth.

* “hvervet os” means enlisted ourselves. It continues the military metaphor.

* “korsfane” is the banner of the cross. It belongs to older Christian hymnological and military imagery.

* “selvlagede vaaben” means self-made weapons: methods or instruments devised from human resources rather than received from God.

* “vidnesbyrd” means testimony or witness-bearing. In this ecclesial context it refers to spoken proclamation in a meeting, not merely personal autobiography.

* “aand og ild” means spirit and fire: vitality, zeal, conviction, and urgency. It should not be rendered as mere enthusiasm.

* “vækkelse” must be rendered consistently as “awakening.” Here it denotes spiritual awakening but is explicitly distinguished from conversion and life in God. “Revival” would often suggest a broader corporate event and would blur this distinction.

* “smyger sine bugtegange” evokes the winding, creeping motion of a serpent or concealed evil power. The image must not be flattened into “works among people.”

* “gjennembrudstider” means seasons of breakthrough in which spiritual life breaks through inertia and becomes effective.

* “livsytringer” means manifestations or expressions of life.

* “opofrelse” means sacrifice or self-sacrifice, not simply effort.

* “aandelige rørelser” means spiritual stirrings or movements. The word does not by itself guarantee that the movement is genuine.

* “oprusning” is a transient excitation or intoxication. In this context it describes emotional stimulation that resembles spiritual life but lacks enduring substance.

* “uimottagelig” means unreceptive or impervious.

* “sindsforandring” is literally a change of mind or inner disposition. It has affinities with repentance, but should not automatically be collapsed into “repentance,” since the author also uses “omvendelse,” conversion.

* “oms-kape” means to recreate or refashion, not merely to improve.

* “aandfyldte” means Spirit-filled. The reference is to the Holy Spirit, not merely to spirited or inspiring speech.

* “avsætte sin ... frugt” means to yield or bear fruit. It continues the agricultural imagery.

* “sløvhet” denotes spiritual dullness, torpor, or insensibility.

* “halve, kraftløse gudsfrygt” is half-hearted, powerless godliness. “Gudsfrygt” literally means fear of God but here denotes lived piety or godliness.

* “undlot os” appears contextually to represent or reflect the older expression “undslog os,” meaning that we declined, drew back, or refused.

* “forsagelse” means renunciation or self-denial, especially voluntary relinquishment for Christ’s sake.

* “blodsdaaben” means the baptism of blood: Christ’s suffering and death on the cross.

* “gjennemglødes” means to be heated or made incandescent throughout. It conveys complete inward penetration by grief and zeal.

* “kampfærdig erobringsaand” means a battle-ready spirit of conquest. The phrase is missionary rather than political or coercive: its stated aim is to win the people for Christ.

* “ordets skarpe refselse” is the word’s sharp rebuke or reproof. Its cutting moral force must remain intact.

Syntactic Hazards

* The prose relies on long cumulative sentences in which thought develops through causal clauses, qualifications, parallel phrases, and semicolon-like pivots. Dividing these into short modern sentences would weaken the argument.

* The repeated connectors “Ti,” “Men,” “Og,” “Derfor,” and “Saaledes” establish explicit logical movement. “Ti” should generally be rendered “For,” retaining the author’s explanatory logic.

* Several sentences postpone their main clause while accumulating descriptive material. This is especially important in the passage concerning those who long for spring, watch for morning, and speak with burning hearts.

* The author often moves from assertion to rhetorical question and then to answer. These questions are structural, not ornamental.

* The passage contrasts apparent spiritual movement with genuine conversion through carefully ordered qualifications. The sequence must not be rearranged.

* The sentence beginning “Og der hvor Herrens sandhet...” appears textually disrupted in the supplied transcription. The most coherent reading is that the Lord’s truth is spoken by living, Spirit-filled witnesses in a meeting prepared and conducted under earnest prayer.

* Biblical quotations are embedded directly within the reasoning. They should not be isolated as decorative epigraphs.

* The contrast between “følelseslivet” and “viljelivet” must remain within a single argumentative unit.

* The final paragraph deliberately moves from hidden inward awakening to outwardly visible life and work. That progression must be preserved.

Theological Hazards

* Awakening is not treated as equivalent to conversion. The author explicitly states that awakening may be only a beginning.

* Emotional movement is not accepted as proof of the Holy Spirit’s work.

* Genuine conversion involves the word, the agency of God’s Spirit, a change of mind, and new life in God.

* The word of the Lord possesses intrinsic power to recreate human hearts. The translation must not imply that this power originates in the speaker’s ability.

* Prayer, work, sacrifice, and preparation are necessary, but they do not mechanically produce spiritual life. The decisive agent is God’s Spirit working through the word.

* The spiritual awakening of believers is presented as the highest fruit of a meeting, because awakened believers become instruments in the salvation of others.

* “Half-hearted, powerless godliness” is not a minor weakness. It may constitute a major obstacle to the kingdom of God and to the salvation of souls.

* Christian discipleship is defined through self-denial, suffering, bearing the cross, and self-forgetting labor for Christ’s cause.

* Christ’s death excludes a merely recreational, conversational, or socially comfortable form of religion.

* The imagery of conquest must remain subordinated to the aim of winning people for Christ through testimony and transformed life.

Voice Hazards

* The author is severe toward superficial preaching but warm toward sincere longing for awakening. These tonal differences must remain distinct.

* The passage must not sound like modern advice about public speaking. Preparation is framed as spiritual obedience and warfare.

* The author speaks directly to the conscience of Christian witnesses. The translation must preserve implication and accountability.

* The praise of awakening must be full and sincere before the author introduces its limitations. Premature qualification would weaken the rhetorical fairness of the argument.

* The apple-blossom image is gentle and beautiful, but its purpose is morally searching: promise is not fruit.

* The rebuke of believers must retain its humiliating force, especially in the admission that Christ has offered opportunities to bear the cross and believers have declined.

* The critique of religious meetings as occasions for enjoyment must not be softened into a general warning against distraction.

* The final account of hidden awakening is quiet rather than spectacular, but not weak. Its inwardness intensifies rather than diminishes its seriousness.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “En ‘fegten i veiret’” echoes the biblical image of fighting or beating the air. It describes activity that has the appearance of combat but strikes no adversary and accomplishes nothing. “Beating the air” is the natural English biblical equivalent.

* “Finde ind i ordet” literally means to find one’s way into the word. The phrase implies laboring until the speaker has entered into its meaning and truth personally. A merely literal “find into the word” would be obscure.

* “Føre vore tilhørere ind i det” extends the preceding image. The speaker first enters the word and then seeks to lead the hearers into it.

* “Sætte vor ære i” means to regard something as one’s honor or ground of glory. It expresses a settled value, not momentary pride.

* “Bære Jesus Kristus til skue” means to bring Christ openly into view or hold him forth before the hearers. “To hold Jesus Christ forth to view” preserves both the visual image and the sermonic dignity.

* “Under hvis korsfane vi kjæmper” belongs to traditional Christian military language: believers fight under the sign and authority of Christ’s cross.

* “At tale for at tale” means speaking merely for the sake of speaking, without a defined spiritual aim.

* “Der er aand og ild over dem” means that their speech bears evident spiritual fervor and force. A literal “there is spirit and fire over them” would be unidiomatic in English.

* “De sovende maa vækkes” belongs to the widespread biblical and pietistic contrast between spiritual sleep and awakening.

* “Smyger sine bugtegange” evokes something serpentine, concealed, and winding. The evil powers do not advance openly but insinuate themselves through crooked courses.

* “Vaarens livsytringer” means the visible signs or manifestations of life in spring. It contrasts with the preceding winter cold and spiritual sleep.

* “Øine ... trætte av at speide efter morgenen” echoes the biblical watchman waiting for morning. It expresses prolonged spiritual expectation rather than ordinary visual fatigue.

* “Taarevædte” gives visible bodily form to sorrow. It should remain “wet with tears” or “tear-wet,” not merely “sad.”

* “Epleblomsten om vaaren” represents an encouraging beginning that may or may not mature. The blossom is not condemned; it is denied the status of completed fruit.

* “Aandens verden” means the spiritual realm or the sphere of the Spirit. “The world of the Spirit” best preserves the original form.

* “Overfladisk oprusning” is an emotional intoxication that produces temporary excitement without inward transformation.

* “Avsætte sin ... frugt” uses the language of producing or bearing fruit. It has broad biblical associations with the fruits of faith, repentance, and the word.

* “Ta korset op” is the established biblical expression for accepting the cost of discipleship.

* “Verdens lys” is a direct biblical designation of Christ’s disciples.

* “Gjennem blodsdaaben paa korset” connects Christ’s death with his description of his coming suffering as a baptism.

* “Samle skatte paa jorden” is the biblical image of laying up earthly treasures in opposition to heavenly treasure.

* “Gjennemglødes” depicts the congregation as penetrated throughout by heat. The heat consists of sorrow over spiritual need and energetic readiness for conflict.

* “Stormende bevægelse” means a tumultuous, conspicuous, or emotionally overwhelming movement. It is contrasted with the quiet but real work of the Holy Spirit.

* “Ordets skarpe refselse” depicts the word as morally piercing. It is not merely criticism offered by a speaker.

King James Bible Equivalents and Their Use

* “Beating the air” is a natural inherited equivalent for “fegten i veiret,” though the surrounding translation should not adopt archaic pronouns or wholesale King James diction.

* “The old has passed away; behold, all has become new” retains the structure and solemnity of the biblical declaration without using obsolete grammar.

* “They all slumbered and fell asleep” closely preserves the familiar structure of Matthew 25:5.

* “Will you also go away?” is already natural modern English and preserves the gravity of John 6:67.

* “The light of the world,” “take up the cross,” and “lay up treasures on earth” are established biblical expressions and should be retained.

* Luke 12:49 may be rendered with the original fronting: “Fire I have come to cast upon the earth,” allowing the force to arise from syntax rather than archaism.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Thesis concerning preparation:
  No participant in Christian witness should shrink from the labor required for preparation.

* First supporting claim:
  Preparation benefits the witness’s own spiritual development and readiness for conflict with the enemy.

* Governing condition:
  Anyone who wants participation in the Lord’s army to achieve something more than beating the air must accept the cost of toil, work, and prayer.

* Warning against false measures of success:
  A speaker may obtain recognition or praise without having entered deeply into the word or led the hearers into it.

* Identification of the false goal:
  Some hearers and speakers regard emotional stimulation as the desired yield of a meeting.

* Moral correction:
  Human praise and blame must carry little weight. The true aim is to hold Christ forth and receive the approval of the exalted king.

* Further limitation:
  Christ’s approval cannot be gained through self-invented weapons or the pursuit of personal honor.

* Second major thesis:
  Every person who bears testimony in a meeting must have both an aim and a plan.

* Negative example:
  Some speakers intend nothing beyond speaking itself and therefore misuse the meeting’s time.

* More serious example:
  Other speakers possess evident spirit and fire and do intend something, namely awakening.

* Affirmation:
  The desire for awakening is right and necessary. The author rejoices in testimony arising from grief over spiritual sleep and longing for renewed life.

* Expansion of the need:
  Individuals and the Lord’s congregation continually need seasons of renewed spiritual effectiveness.

* Pastoral encouragement:
  The author refuses to dampen the zeal of those who long, pray, work, sacrifice, and testify for awakening.

* First major qualification:
  Even a genuine spiritual movement, including requests for prayer and guidance, is not enough.

* Doctrinal distinction:
  Awakening is a promising beginning but is not identical with conversion or life in God.

* Agricultural illustration:
  Apple blossoms are beautiful and promising, but summer and harvest reveal whether they become fruit.

* Second major qualification:
  Some apparent spiritual movements are not genuine at all.

* Warning of negative consequence:
  Superficial excitement may produce nothing or may harden the person and make that person less receptive to truth.

* Positive definition of the proper aim:
  Testimony must reach not only the emotions but also the will, so that God’s Spirit may work conversion, change of mind, and new life in God through the word.

* Appeal to experience:
  Large movements have often failed to produce enduring change.

* Diagnosis:
  Where the apparent change ends with the meeting, the testimony itself was not what it ought to have been.

* Doctrinal reassurance:
  The Lord’s word still possesses power to recreate human hearts.

* Necessary conditions of faithful proclamation:
  The meeting is prepared and conducted under earnest prayer, and the truth is spoken by living, Spirit-filled witnesses.

* Revaluation of visible results:
  The word will bear blessed fruit even where no large awakening is outwardly observed.

* Climactic claim:
  The highest fruit of a meeting is that believers themselves are awakened.

* Anticipated objection:
  Believers may already be awake.

* Response:
  They may fall asleep again and therefore continually need to be awakened to their own dullness and the peril of others.

* Intensified warning:
  The half-hearted and powerless godliness of professing Christians may be the greatest obstacle to the kingdom of God and the salvation of souls.

* Appeal to conscience:
  Christ has repeatedly offered believers opportunities to take up the cross, and they have declined.

* Twofold witness:
  This failure is seen both by Christ and by those who expect Christians to be the light of the world.

* Definition of the church’s public testimony:
  The congregation’s life must testify through voluntary renunciation, suffering, and self-forgetting labor for Christ.

* Christological rebuke:
  Christ did not give his life through the baptism of blood so that believers might reduce religion to discussion, comfortable gatherings, private ease, and earthly accumulation.

* Concluding missionary appeal:
  The necessary fire will be kindled when God’s congregation awakens, sees the danger, and is penetrated by grief and a glad, battle-ready zeal to win the people for Christ.

* Final clarification:
  Such awakening may occur without tumultuous outward movement.

* Concluding pastoral observation:
  The awakening of believers is often initially hidden from everyone except the Lord and the soul being awakened by the word’s rebuke.

* Visible confirmation:
  The inward awakening will later become evident in renewed and enriched godliness expressed through life and work.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* “Fegten i veiret”:
  1 Corinthians 9:26. Paul says that he does not fight as one beating the air.

* The Lord’s fighting army, enlistment, weapons, and warfare:
  The imagery broadly echoes Ephesians 6:10–17, 2 Timothy 2:3–4, and other New Testament descriptions of Christian warfare.

* “Bære Jesus Kristus til skue”:
  Possibly Galatians 3:1, where Jesus Christ is described as having been publicly set forth before the hearers.

* Sleep and awakening:
  Ephesians 5:14; Romans 13:11; 1 Thessalonians 5:6; and the parable of the ten virgins in Matthew 25.

* Watching for the morning:
  Psalm 130:5–6, where the soul waits for the Lord more than watchmen wait for the morning.

* Spring, blossom, and fruit:
  The image is not a direct quotation but participates in the biblical language of fruit in Matthew 3:8–10, Matthew 7:16–20, John 15:1–8, and related passages.

* Conversion, change of mind, and new life:
  The conceptual field includes Acts 2:38, Acts 3:19, Romans 6, and Ephesians 4:22–24.

* “Det gamle er forbigangent, se alt er blitt nyt”:
  2 Corinthians 5:17.

* “Da brudgommen gav sig tid, slumret de alle og sov ind”:
  Matthew 25:5.

* “Ta korset op”:
  Matthew 16:24; Mark 8:34; Luke 9:23.

* “Vil ogsaa I gaa bort?”:
  John 6:67.

* “Verdens lys”:
  Matthew 5:14.

* The testimony given by the congregation’s life:
  Matthew 5:16; Philippians 2:15; 1 Peter 2:12.

* “Blodsdaaben”:
  Mark 10:38–39 and Luke 12:50, where Jesus describes his suffering as a baptism.

* “Samle skatte paa jorden”:
  Matthew 6:19–21.

* “Ild er jeg kommet for at kaste paa jorden”:
  Luke 12:49.

* “Uavladelig smerte” over the people:
  Likely an echo of Romans 9:2, where Paul speaks of great sorrow and unceasing anguish in his heart for his people.

* The word’s sharp rebuke:
  Hebrews 4:12–13 and 2 Timothy 3:16.

* Renewal manifested in life:
  Romans 12:2; Ephesians 4:23–24; James 2:17–18.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* “No one among us should shrink back” preserves the moral summons and avoids the weaker “need not be afraid.”

* “Preparation is necessary” retains the force of “maa der forberedelse til.”

* The description of emotional results as easily obtained “without preparation and without prayer” must remain blunt.

* “Human praise or human blame should have little influence upon us” retains the balanced judgment and refuses psychological softening.

* The rebuke of purposeless speakers remains categorical: a person who intends nothing should never occupy the meeting’s time.

* The praise of those longing for awakening remains generous, so that the subsequent correction cannot be mistaken for hostility toward awakening itself.

* “It is not enough” must stand without qualification after the description of visible spiritual response.

* The possibility that emotional excitement may produce “worse than nothing” must remain intact.

* The claim that deficient testimony may explain transient results must not be softened into a mere possibility of poor technique.

* The warning concerning “half-hearted, powerless godliness” must retain its implication that professing Christians may obstruct the salvation of others.

* “We declined” preserves the shame of refusing opportunities to bear the cross.

* Christ’s death is placed in direct opposition to comfortable religious sociability and earthly accumulation. The antithesis must remain severe.

* The final missionary language should be energetic but not theatrical. The force comes from the necessity of awakening and the consequences of spiritual sleep.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* “bør ingen ... ræddes tilbake”:
  “No one ... should shrink back.” This is an ethical obligation, not a suggestion.

* “maa der forberedelse til”:
  “Preparation is necessary.” The necessity is unconditional once the stated aim is accepted.

* “bør ha liten indflydelse”:
  “Should have little influence.” The speaker states a norm for Christian conduct.

* “bør aldrig være”:
  “Should never be.” The prohibition remains categorical.

* “burde aldrig opta møtets tid”:
  “Should never occupy the meeting’s time.” The original moral judgment is preserved.

* “de sovende maa vækkes”:
  “That those who sleep may be awakened.” Here “maa” expresses the desired result within a clause of longing, not a command directed to the sleepers.

* “Gud maa velsigne”:
  “May God bless.” This is prayerful or optative force.

* “kan bli omvendelse”:
  “So that there may be conversion.” The possibility depends explicitly upon the working of God’s Spirit through the word.

* “skulde ha”:
  “Ought to have had” or “was meant to have.” It expresses failure to meet the proper norm.

* “vil ... avsætte”:
  “Will always bear.” The author makes a confident assertion concerning the Lord’s truth.

* “trænger ... at vækkes”:
  “Continually need to be awakened.” This is genuine necessity.

* “tør hænde”:
  “It may indeed be.” The phrase introduces a grave possibility, not a remote speculation.

* “skal være ‘verdens lys’”:
  “Are to be the light of the world.” The phrase expresses vocation and obligation.

* “kan vindes”:
  “Can be won only.” The exclusivity must remain.

* “for at vi skal diskutere”:
  “So that we should discuss.” The construction defines a purpose emphatically denied by the author.

* “kan tændes”:
  “Can be kindled.” The enabling condition is the awakening of the congregation.

* “vilde den alt var tændt”:
  “How gladly I would have it already kindled.” This preserves intense desire without archaic pronouns.

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Military metaphor:
  The Lord’s army, combat-readiness, enemy, enlistment, banner, weapons, battle-ready spirit, and conquest are retained as one coherent field of imagery.

* Futile combat:
  “Beating the air” remains an image rather than being paraphrased as ineffectiveness.

* Yield and fruit:
  The “yield” of a meeting leads into blossom and fruit imagery. This agricultural field is preserved.

* Sleep and awakening:
  Spiritual sleep, awakening, and falling asleep again remain concrete metaphors.

* Seasonal imagery:
  Winter cold, spring’s manifestations of life, summer, and harvest preserve the progression from dormancy through promise to fulfilled fruit.

* Night and morning:
  The disappearance of night and watching for morning retain the eschatological and devotional resonance of longing.

* Apple blossom:
  The blossom remains beautiful and genuinely promising, though not a guarantee of fruit.

* Recreated heart:
  The Lord’s word does not merely influence but recreates human hearts.

* Cross-bearing:
  The cross remains the concrete sign of costly discipleship.

* Baptism of blood:
  Christ’s death retains its sacramental and passion imagery.

* Light:
  The church’s life is expected to function as the light of the world.

* Fire:
  The fire cast upon the earth is retained as Christ’s own image and linked to the awakening of the congregation.

* Incandescence:
  The congregation is “penetrated throughout” by unceasing pain and missionary zeal, preserving the force of being made to glow from within.

* Outward imprint:
  Renewed godliness marks or stamps the believer’s outward life and work.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* The opening combines sober demand with encouragement: preparation is costly, yet it yields spiritual growth and readiness.

* Human praise is contrasted with the approving glance of Christ.

* The exposure of superficial emotionalism is followed by warm affirmation of sincere longing for awakening.

* The winter imagery is severe, while the longing for spring and morning introduces hope.

* The beauty of apple blossom is affirmed before its insufficiency is stated.

* Genuine awakening is distinguished from both conversion and counterfeit excitement.

* Confidence in the power of the Lord’s word follows the humiliating account of failed meetings.

* The claim that believers may hinder the kingdom intensifies the severity of the passage.

* Christ’s attentive and concerned gaze introduces personal pastoral gravity within the rebuke.

* The exposure of comfortable religion is followed by an energetic vision of an awakened, missionary congregation.

* Tumultuous outward response is contrasted with the hidden, inward awakening known at first only to the Lord and the soul.

* The closing movement is hopeful: hidden renewal will become visible in enriched godliness and outward work.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Long sentences are retained where the author builds pressure by accumulation.

* Short rhetorical questions remain isolated:
  “But what do they want?”
  “Is this, then, not right?”

* Repeated openings with “For,” “But,” “And,” and “No” preserve the sermonic movement.

* The extended sentence concerning those who long for spring retains its multiple descriptive clauses before reaching the main assertion.

* Parallel verbal sequences are preserved:
  “lament and long, pray and labor, testify and act.”

* The apple-blossom paragraph retains short declarative units, allowing the image to unfold orally.

* Biblical quotations remain woven into the spoken argument.

* The severe sentence concerning Christ’s baptism of blood retains its long accumulation of rejected religious behaviors.

* The final paragraphs slow the pace, moving from public awakening to hidden inward rebuke and then to outwardly visible renewal.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION

Some Words Concerning the Lutheran Free Church and Its Tasks

E. B.

[ornament]

VI.

And here no one among us should shrink back from the labor and exertion that such preparation requires. For it repays us well in our own spiritual development and in our fitness for battle against the enemy. If we truly desire that our participation in the Lord’s fighting army should yield some result, should be something other and more than a “beating of the air,” then preparation is necessary—regardless of what it costs in toil, labor, and prayer. I know very well that we can obtain recognition, and perhaps even praise, from some people without laboring with great exertion to find our own way into the word and without seeking to lead our hearers into it. For what some seek to obtain as the yield of a meeting is that the emotional life of the soul should in some way be set in motion. And the preacher who speaks in such a manner that this is attained often receives praise from superficial persons. But everyone knows that such results can easily be attained without preparation and without prayer—and many are tempted to be satisfied with such things. This, however, is not, and should never be, what we aim at when we participate in a meeting by bearing testimony. Human praise or human blame should have little influence upon us. Our calling is to set our honor in “holding Jesus Christ forth to view,” whether this brings us censure or recognition. For what is worth more than human praise or human blame is to receive a friendly, approving glance from him, our great and exalted king, in whose army we have enlisted and under whose banner of the cross we fight.

But his approval is never gained by fighting with self-made weapons, nor by seeking honor for ourselves.

Another matter to which I would also gladly point, because in my judgment it is so significant, is that the person who participates in a meeting should have an aim and a plan for his testimony: an aim, so that he intends something, and a plan, so that he pursues his aim.

We need not listen long to the testimony of some before it becomes clear to us that, at bottom, they aim at nothing and particularly desire nothing, except perhaps this: to speak for the sake of speaking.

That this is wrong should be evident to everyone. And the person who intends nothing by his speech should never occupy the meeting’s time. There are others who speak, and in whose testimony it can plainly be perceived that they do intend something. There are spirit and fire in them.

But what do they intend?

Some of them are almost offended by such a question and answer with indignation: What do we intend? We intend awakening. We intend that the people to whom we speak should be awakened. Yes, is this, then, not right? Certainly it is. And I rejoice every time that, in a meeting, I am permitted to hear a testimony borne forth by a heart filled with a burning desire that those who sleep may be awakened.

[Page 290, column 2]

For awakening is continually needed, so long as the evil powers in the world wind their creeping courses around human beings.

The individual and the Lord’s congregation need awakening—such seasons of breakthrough in which life is renewed and made effective. And I will never join those who would dampen the zeal of those whose testimony aims at awakening.

No, let the word of awakening sound forth with clarity and power in our meetings! For the winter cold and the sleepiness are easily perceived everywhere. And when those who long for the manifestations of spring’s life, for the disappearance of the night—those whose eyes are weary from watching for the morning and wet with tears from sorrow over themselves and their brothers and sisters in the world—when such persons, with burning and throbbing hearts, speak for awakening in a meeting, then I rejoice. And from my innermost heart I desire that God may bless all those who are grieved over the spiritual distress among our people, and who therefore lament and long, pray and labor, testify and act, that awakening may come! For as a rule it comes only through labor, sacrifice, and prayer. But even if it comes, even if—as I have already pointed out—there arises some more or less powerful spiritual movement, and even if one or more persons indicate that they desire intercessory prayer and guidance, it is not enough. And the testimony that aims only at awakening, only at movement, is imperfect and one-sided.

Spiritual movement, where it is genuine, is only a promising beginning, and nothing more. The apple blossom in spring is beautiful; that is true. But the blossom is not thereby a guarantee of fruit. What is decisive is the summer and the harvest. Then it will become evident whether the blossom became more than a promising possibility. So it is also in the world of the Spirit. Awakening may be promising, but it is not the same as conversion and life in God.

But at times the spiritual stirrings and movements produced by a testimony in a meeting are not even genuine. The movement may be only a superficial intoxication that results in nothing, or in something worse than nothing: it makes the person harder and less receptive to the truth than before. It is therefore so important that the person who speaks for awakening in a meeting should set as his aim that the souls whom he seeks to reach through his testimony should be affected not only in their emotional life, but also in their volitional life, so that, through the working of God’s Spirit by means of the word, there may be conversion, change of mind, and new life in God. Only then has the testimony produced the effect that it ought to have.

It has been shown, not once but many times, that great movements in a meeting fulfilled very little of what they promised. The change of mind that appeared to be traceable in several persons lasted, in many cases, no longer than until the end of the emotionally charged meeting. And then it is certain that the very testimony that was spoken was not what it ought to have been.

The Lord’s word now, as before, has power in itself to recreate human hearts, so that it may truthfully be said of them: “The old has passed away; behold, all has become new.”

And where the Lord’s truth is spoken by living, Spirit-filled witnesses in a meeting prepared and conducted under earnest prayer, it will always bear its blessed fruit, even though no greater awakening is perceived.

For the highest fruit a meeting can bear is that believers are awakened. Someone may perhaps say that believers are awake. Yes, perhaps they are. But they can fall asleep again: “When the bridegroom delayed, they all slumbered and fell asleep.” And therefore they continually need to be awakened further—awakened to see their own dullness and the great spiritual peril of their fellow human beings. It may indeed be that the greatest obstacle to the kingdom of God, to “the salvation of souls,” is the half-hearted, powerless godliness of those who confess Christ. Is it not a truth established by experience, yet deeply humiliating, that our dear Savior has often offered us an opportunity to “take up the cross,” but—we declined?

It did not escape the attention of him whose solicitous gaze is fixed upon us and who asks with concern: “Will you also go away?”

And perhaps it did not escape the attention of those who expect us to be “the light of the world,” and who can be won for Jesus Christ only through the testimony given by the life of God’s congregation—the life lived in voluntary renunciation and suffering, and in continual, self-forgetting labor for his cause. Christ has never, through the baptism of blood upon the cross, given his life for the salvation of the world so that we should discuss religious questions, attend festivals or meetings in order to enjoy ourselves, and otherwise remain as inconspicuous as possible—“laying up treasures upon earth.”

No, what is needed in order that the fire may be kindled of which Jesus said, “Fire I have come to cast upon the earth, and how gladly I would have it already kindled,” is that God’s congregation should awaken—should see its own danger and the danger of others, and should be penetrated throughout by an “unceasing pain” over the distress of our people and by a glad, battle-ready spirit of conquest whose aim is to win our people for Christ. And where such results are attained through a meeting, there God’s Spirit, who is the true power able to produce awakening, has been at work through the word, even though no tumultuous movement could be perceived.

For the awakening of believers most commonly takes place in such a manner—at the beginning, at least—that no one other than the Lord and the individual soul awakened by the word’s sharp rebuke knows anything of it.

This will, of course, soon be perceived by others as well, since the believer’s outward life and work will bear the mark of the renewal and enrichment of godliness.
